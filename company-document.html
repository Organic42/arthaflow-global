PART 01

Company Overview

Executive Summary

ArthaFlow Global is an AI-powered export operating system built for India's 57 million MSME manufacturers — the 99.7% who don't currently export because the infrastructure doesn't exist for them. We combine an enterprise dashboard, two flagship AI products (an AI Document Generator and Export Saathi — an AI advisor in Hindi, Marathi & English), and a partner network for warehousing, freight, finance and insurance, in one platform priced for the MSME.

Founded in March 2026 by two student-engineers from KJ Somaiya College of Engineering, ArthaFlow is bootstrapped to a live MVP, 5 active pilot manufacturers across Mumbai and Pune, and 1 signed strategic partnership. We are raising ₹50L–2 Cr in a pre-seed round to convert pilots to paying customers, acquire export licenses, and scale across Maharashtra before expanding to Gujarat and Tamil Nadu.

Founding Story

ArthaFlow began with a question: why does India produce world-class manufactured goods yet capture only 2% of global trade? After 50+ direct conversations with MSME factory owners across Maharashtra's industrial belts — Pune, Mumbai, Nashik, Aurangabad — the answer became clear. It wasn't quality. It wasn't ambition. It was infrastructure.

Every manufacturer who tried to export had the same story: weeks lost to documentation, ₹50,000+ spent on consultants for a single shipment, no way to find verified international buyers, and fragmented logistics where one missed step stalled cargo for weeks. Most gave up. Built by founders who lived in the same region as their target customer, ArthaFlow exists to make sure the next 56 million don't have to.

Why we exist

Every product made in India deserves a global market. We're building the rails to get it there.

PART 02

Problem & Market Opportunity

The Problem — 99.7% Don't Export

India has 57 million MSME manufacturers producing export-quality goods. Yet 99.7% never export. The reason is infrastructure: the export process is gated by three compounding barriers that disproportionately punish small manufacturers.

📄 Documentation Nightmare

40+ documents per shipment: IEC, HS codes, shipping bills, COO, proforma invoices. Manual, error-prone, requires ₹50K+ consultants. Most manufacturers give up here.

🔍 No Buyer Access

Zero visibility into who's buying, where, or how to reach verified international buyers. Listing platforms generate leads but don't enable transactions.

🚢 Logistics Overwhelm

Freight, customs, insurance, incoterms — 15+ decisions per shipment. One mistake stalls cargo for weeks and triggers demurrage fees.

Manufacturer Voice, Pune

"I spent 2 years trying to figure out how to export. I gave up after the documentation."

Why Now — Four Converging Tailwinds

The export opportunity isn't new. The ability to capture it — at MSME scale, profitably — is. Four things became true in the last 24 months:

🏛️ Policy Mandate

Government targeting $2T exports by 2030. District Export Hubs, RoDTEP and PLI are funded national policy — pushing MSMEs outward.

🌐 China + 1 Shift

Global buyers de-risking from China. India is the #1 alternative, but buyers can't find verified Indian suppliers. Demand searching for supply.

🤖 AI Inflection

Documentation that needed a ₹50K consultant 24 months ago now costs near-zero via LLMs. Core friction became automatable for the first time.

📱 MSME Digitization

Post-UPI, GST, ONDC, India's smallest manufacturers are digital-native. Nashik factory owners run businesses on smartphones.

Customer Persona — The Buyer

Demographics: Factory owner or operations head, 35–55 years old, primarily Hindi/Marathi/Gujarati speaker. WhatsApp-native, smartphone-first. Has tried export once and given up — or never tried because the documentation maze felt insurmountable.

Firmographics: 10–500 employees, ₹1–50 Cr annual turnover, established 5+ years, GST-registered, located in tier-2/3 manufacturing clusters (Pune, Mumbai, Nashik, Aurangabad, Surat, Coimbatore).

The strategic insight

Software is the hook. Data is the moat. Anyone can copy our UI in a quarter. No one can copy years of verified shipment, pricing, and payment data.

Every transaction on the platform deposits structured data: verified manufacturer profiles, realized prices by HS code by destination, payment behavior on buyers, certification trails. This dataset later powers credit underwriting (Act 3, Year 4+), market intelligence (sold to banks and NBFCs), and a data-driven trading entity.

The Moat — Why We Can't Be Copied

📊 Data Asset

Durable, compounding. Verified manufacturer profiles + realized shipment history. Powers credit underwriting and data-driven trading later. Competitors rebuild from zero.

🔄 Two-Sided Network

Self-reinforcing. Each manufacturer makes the buyer side stronger; each buyer makes supply richer. Liquidity resists displacement at scale.

🧠 Feedback-Tuned AI

Data is the moat, not the model. Saathi and Doc Generator are correct-by-default because they retrieve structured data competitors can't access.

The kill-shot

A competitor's clock to catch us doesn't even start until they get their first 100 manufacturers shipping. Ours started already — and compounds daily.

Disintermediation Defense

A classic risk for B2B marketplaces: once buyer and seller have each other's contact, they transact off-platform. Our defense:

🧾 Bundle settlement

FIRC generation, forex settlement, trade-finance disbursement all happen through ArthaFlow — making the platform the easiest path, not the avoidable middleman.

🛡️ Inspection + escrow

Buyers prefer SGS-grade inspection + protected payment terms. Going off-platform removes both. Trust is the lock-in.

Plug into DGFT / District Export Hub programs — official validation drives manufacturer trust.

PART 12

Risks & Mitigations

Honest assessment of what could go wrong — and the specific mitigations in place or planned.

Risk Register

HIGH
Premature breadth — building Act 2 features during Act 1

The temptation to build the buyer marketplace, full fulfilment stack, and trading desk in parallel — diluting focus before the core manufacturer dataset accretes.

✓ Mitigation: Hard gate. No buyer-side product until ~100 verified manufacturers with ≥1 documented shipment each. Discipline tracked monthly.
HIGH
Disintermediation — buyers and sellers transacting off-platform

Classic B2B marketplace risk. Once parties have each other's contact, they may transact off-platform to avoid the take-rate.

✓ Mitigation: Bundle settlement (FIRC, forex, trade finance) into ArthaFlow so off-platform transactions lose key services. Trust via inspection + escrow.
MEDIUM
Pilot-to-paid conversion at scale unproven

5 active pilots is a small sample. Conversion rate from free pilot to paid Growth tier is the make-or-break Year 1 metric.

✓ Mitigation: 60-day pilots with structured milestones, weekly check-ins, validated pricing from interviews. Track conversion weekly; iterate playbook.
MEDIUM
Regulatory complexity — FEMA, DGFT, customs

Cross-border B2B operations span multiple regulators. Mistakes are expensive and reputationally damaging.

✓ Mitigation: Partner-first for licensed activities (CHA, forex). Bring in-house only when volume + capital justify. Legal advisor on retainer at Pvt Ltd.
MEDIUM
AI commoditization — frontier LLMs erode the document-generation moat

Raw AI document generation will be table-stakes within 18 months. Our differentiation must come from elsewhere.

✓ Mitigation: Moat is the proprietary data feeding the model, not the model itself. Race to build the dataset before AI becomes generic. RAG-first architecture.
MEDIUM
Manufacturer digital adoption resistance

Tier-2/3 manufacturers may resist new tools, especially in English-only formats.

✓ Mitigation: Saathi in Hindi/Marathi removes language barrier. Mobile-first matches existing behavior. In-person onboarding for first 100 customers.
LOW-MED
Vertical heterogeneity — different sectors have wildly different needs

Engineering, pharma, textiles, and agri have different compliance, certifications, and buyer dynamics.

✓ Mitigation: Wedge with ONE vertical (engineering goods) for Year 1. Templatize, then expand. Depth before breadth.
LOW-MED
Capital runway / fundraise timing

Bootstrapped operation has limited runway. Failure to close pre-seed by Q3 2026 forces difficult trade-offs.

✓ Mitigation: Multiple parallel paths (grants, angels, accelerators, equity). Zero-burn ops by default. Revenue from first paid pilots Q3 2026.
LOW
Buyer-side trust & dispute resolution

Once we sit in the middle of cross-border transactions, we own disputes — payment defaults, quality claims, returns.

✓ Mitigation: Inspection partner (SGS/BV) + escrow-style settlement must precede shipment volume. Don't intermediate transactions we can't adjudicate.
